Dynamic Creative: Optional Add-On to CBO

Dynamic Creative is not a strategy by itself. It is an optional toggle you can add to a CBO campaign. When enabled, you upload individual assets (3-5 videos, 5 headlines, 5 body copy) and the platform mixes and matches them into combinations. The algorithm finds which combinations perform best and prioritizes them.

When to add it: Use Dynamic Creative when you are in data collection mode and want the platform to find winning combinations for you. It maximizes testing throughput from a single ad set.

When to skip it: If you have proven creatives and want to control which specific ads run, use individual ads instead. Dynamic Creative gives the platform control over combinations. Individual ads give you control.

Never use Dynamic Creative during low-trust periods. The platform's auto-enhancement toggles that come with Dynamic Creative can alter your creative without consent. During low trust, switch to individual ads and turn all enhancement toggles off.

Conversion Events Explained

Before a CSM recommends a strategy, they need to understand what conversion event the campaign optimizes around. This is the action the ad platform uses to determine who to show ads to.

What Is a Conversion Event?

A conversion event is the specific action the ad platform optimizes around. When you set up a conversion campaign, you tell the platform: find me more people likely to take THIS action. The platform uses its database of user behavior history to find people statistically probable to do that thing.

The event you choose determines who the algorithm targets. If you optimize for Link Clicks, you get clickers. If you optimize for Leads, you get people likely to fill out a form. If you optimize for Schedule, you get people likely to book a call. The event is the signal the algorithm chases.

There are two categories. Standard events are predefined by the platform: Lead, Complete Registration, Schedule, Purchase, Contact, Submit Form, Find Location, Start Trial. The platform has historical data on every user who has ever taken these actions across all advertisers. Custom conversions are defined by you and linked to a specific URL or event. They can be useful but the platform has no historical data on them beyond what your pixel has collected, so optimization is weaker.

The rule: Default to standard events whenever possible. The platform has a gigantic database of all their active users and the actions they have historically taken or not taken. You want to play into that database rather than using a custom conversion where the platform has no historical data beyond what your business has accumulated in isolation. Standard events typically produce a far lower cost per result and a far greater quantity of results.

Purchase

Structure: Two campaigns. Front-end optimizes for Purchase. Retargeting optimizes for Schedule. AOV: Add upsells at checkout to increase order value and liquidate more ad spend on the front end.

Cost Per Application vs Cost Per Qualified Call

These are two different metrics. Confusing them leads to bad decisions.

Cost per application is what you pay when a prospect submits the form on your VSL page. For high-ticket fitness VSL funnels this runs $15-$45 cold. That is the front-end metric before the scheduler, before qualification filtering, before drop-off.

Cost per qualified call is what you pay for a prospect who actually makes it through the scheduler, passes qualification, and books a call. For high-ticket fitness VSL funnels this runs $150-$250 cold because of the drop-off between application and scheduled call. Every step between form submit and booked call shrinks the pool, which inflates the cost per person who actually makes it through.

What inflates cost per qualified call above cost per application: The drop-off rate between application and scheduler. If 100 people apply at $20 each ($2,000 total) but only 30% schedule a call, your cost per scheduled call is $67, not $20. If only 15% of those scheduled calls are qualified, your cost per qualified call is $133. This is why cost per qualified call is always higher than cost per application. Track both. The gap between them tells you where the leak is.

How to lower cost per qualified call: Fix pixel conditioning so you are only sending qualified conversions back to the platform. Remove unnecessary funnel steps between application and scheduler. Shorten the form. Add same-day SMS follow-up to people who apply but do not schedule. Each of these tightens the gap between cost per application and cost per qualified call.

How to Use These Benchmarks

These ranges are for high-ticket fitness offers specifically. The ranges are wide because the actual CPA depends on the offer price point, the creative quality, the audience saturation in the fitness niche, and the market competition at the time of running.

What to tell clients: These are guardrails, not targets. If your cold VSL call CPA is at $200, that is solid. If it climbs to $300 and your show rate holds, something changed in the auction or your creative fatigued. If it climbs to $300 and show rate drops, your creative is attracting the wrong people. The CPA number alone does not diagnose the problem. It tells you something is off. The show rate, close rate, and call quality tell you what.

What moves the CPA: CPMs (auction competition), link click-through rate (creative quality), opt-in rate (page quality), and the conversion event itself. If your CPA is high, break it down into those components before changing anything. Do not just swap creatives. Find which sub-metric is broken first.

Transition Protocol: Moving Between Strategies

When a client's spend or conversion volume changes, they may need to move from one strategy to another. These are the exact signals that trigger each transition and the steps to execute it without breaking the account.

Strategy A: CBO >>> Strategy B: ABO + Individual
When this transition fires: The client has been on CBO long enough to identify proven hooks and their conversion volume has crossed 10+ per week. They are spending $300-$1K/day and have proven hooks from the CBO testing phase. The signal is that CBO is no longer the right structure because you now have enough data to control budget distribution manually and you want to scale proven winners.
Trigger signals: 10+ conversions per week for 2+ consecutive weeks. Proven hooks identified from CBO data. Sales team confirms lead quality is consistent. CPMs are stable. You have survey data from buyers on why they bought.
Steps to execute:
  • Do not touch the existing CBO campaign. Let it keep running while you build the new ABO structure
  • Build the new ABO campaign with 3 cold ad sets: broad, interest stack, lookalike stack
  • Load 25-30 individual ads per ad set using the same 5 headlines and 5 body copy across all ads
  • Set up a separate warm audience campaign with its own warm audience stack
  • Apply cost caps at the ad set level based on the CPA you were achieving in the CBO campaign
  • Let the new ABO campaign run for 72 hours before making any changes
  • Once the ABO campaign is stable and producing calls, pause the old CBO campaign
  • Do not delete the old CBO campaign. Pause it. You may need to reactivate it if the ABO structure underperforms
Do not: Add creatives or ad sets to the existing CBO campaign to turn it into ABO. Adding to a working campaign breaks it. Build a new campaign and transition over.
Strategy B: ABO + Individual >>> Strategy C: Thunderdome
When this transition fires: The client is on ABO + Individual and their creatives are fatiguing. You need to test a new batch of creatives rapidly to find fresh winners. Thunderdome is not a replacement for ABO. It is a testing structure that runs alongside or replaces the testing phase of ABO.
Trigger signals: Cost per result climbing over 2+ weeks with no changes to the campaign. CTR dropping. Frequency climbing above 2.0. The same 1-3 ads are absorbing all spend and the rest are getting nothing. You need new winners but the current structure is not testing efficiently.
Steps to execute:
  • Keep the existing ABO scaling campaign running. Do not pause it
  • Build a new Thunderdome campaign with 15-30+ ad sets, each with 1 ad and its own budget
  • Use broad targeting across all Thunderdome ad sets. Identical audience, different creatives
  • Set 3-second video view exclusions on each ad so prospects rotate through different creatives
  • Judge by leading indicators: CPMs, link CTR, cost per call, call quantity. Then correlate to ROAS
  • Clip losers fast. Any creative above your cost threshold gets cut within 5-7 days
  • Move spend from cut losers toward winners to test their scale ceiling
  • Pull proven winners out of Thunderdome and load them into the ABO scaling campaign as new ads
  • Thunderdome runs continuously as a creative factory. ABO scales what Thunderdome finds
Do not: Run Thunderdome below $1K/day. The budget per ad set will be too thin to read anything. Thunderdome needs enough spend per ad set to get statistically meaningful data on each creative.
Strategy A: CBO >>> Strategy C: Thunderdome
When this transition fires: The client has scaled from low spend to $1K+/day rapidly and needs to test creatives aggressively without going through the intermediate ABO phase. This happens when a client gets funded or decides to scale hard and skips the gradual ramp.
Trigger signals: Spend jumps from under $300/day to $1K+/day in a single budget increase. The client wants to test a high volume of creatives fast. No proven winners yet because the CBO phase was too short to identify them.
Steps to execute:
  • Pause the CBO campaign or let it run alongside Thunderdome if it was producing
  • Build Thunderdome with 15-30+ ad sets at 1 ad each, forced distribution, broad targeting
  • Load every creative you have. This is the rapid testing phase
  • Set 3-second view exclusions so prospects rotate through creatives
  • Run for 7 days minimum before cutting anything. You need enough data per creative
  • Once winners emerge, pull them into a new ABO scaling campaign with the Strategy B structure
  • Keep Thunderdome running to feed new creatives into the pipeline
Do not: Skip the data collection entirely. Even in a rapid scale scenario, you need 7 days of Thunderdome data before you can identify winners. Do not cut creatives before they have had enough spend to read.
Strategy A: CBO (under $100/day) >>> CBO or ABO ($100-$300/day)
When this transition fires: The client has crossed the $100/day threshold. Above $100/day, you are past the borderline stage where splitting was not viable. Now each ad set gets enough budget to read data independently. The client can now split between multiple ad sets and use ABO.
Trigger signals: Daily spend sustained above $100/day for 7+ days. The CBO campaign has identified which targeting angles perform best. The client wants to test multiple ad sets or gain more control over budget distribution.
Steps to execute:
  • If staying on CBO: simply add a second ad set to the existing CBO campaign. The platform will distribute budget between them. This is safe because you are adding to CBO, which auto-distributes
  • If moving to ABO: build a new campaign. Do not convert the CBO campaign to ABO by changing settings. Start fresh
  • For ABO, build 2-3 ad sets: broad, interest stack, and optionally lookalike if you have purchaser data
  • Set ad set budgets so each gets $50-150/day minimum. At $200/day with 2 ad sets, each gets $100. At $300/day with 3, each gets $100
  • Dynamic Creative is still optional here. You can run it on CBO or skip it on ABO and use individual ads
  • Let the new structure run 72 hours before judging
Do not: Add a lookalike stack to ABO until you have enough purchaser data for the platform to model from. At $100-$300/day with under 10 conversions/week, stick to broad + interest. Lookalikes come after you have proven buyers.
Strategy B: ABO + Individual >>> Strategy B+C: ABO Scale + Thunderdome Test
When this transition fires: The client has crossed $3K/day and needs both a stable scaling structure and a continuous creative testing pipeline. At this spend, running only ABO means creative fatigue becomes a constant problem because you are burning through winners faster. Thunderdome running alongside ABO solves this.
Trigger signals: Spend sustained above $3K/day for 2+ weeks. The ABO campaign is stable and producing calls but creatives are fatiguing faster than you can replace them. The client needs a systematic way to feed new winners into the ABO campaign without disrupting it.
Steps to execute:
  • Keep the existing ABO scaling campaign running untouched. Do not modify it
  • Build a new Thunderdome campaign with 15-30+ ad sets at 1 ad each, forced distribution, broad targeting
  • Allocate roughly 20-30% of total spend to Thunderdome, 70-80% to the ABO scaling campaign
  • Thunderdome tests new creatives continuously. Judge by leading indicators for 5-7 days
  • When Thunderdome identifies a winner, pull it out and load it as a new ad in the ABO scaling campaign
  • Do not add the winner to the existing ABO ad sets. Relaunch the ad set with the new winner included, or start a new ad set within the ABO campaign
  • Thunderdome becomes a permanent fixture. It is not a one-time test. It runs continuously
Do not: Allocate more than 30% of total spend to Thunderdome at this stage. Thunderdome has self-competition inflation. You want the majority of spend in the stable ABO campaign. Thunderdome is the R&D arm, ABO is the revenue arm.

Dynamic Creative: Add or Skip?

Add it when: You are in data collection mode and want the platform to find winning combinations for you. Dynamic Creative maximizes testing throughput from a single ad set. Load 3-5 videos, 5 headlines, 5 body copy and let the algorithm mix and match.

Skip it when: You have proven creatives and want to control which specific ads run. Or when platform trust is low and you do not want auto-enhancement toggles altering your creative. Use individual ads instead and keep all enhancement toggles off.

  • At $30-$50/day: one campaign, one broad ad set, Dynamic Creative optional. This is the only structure that gives the algorithm enough signal at that spend level
  • At under $100/day: same structure. $100/day is the minimum viable spend for a single campaign
  • At $100-$300/day: you can now split to 2-3 ad sets. CBO handles distribution between them. ABO becomes available as an alternative
  • If using Dynamic Creative: mix majority and minority hooks so the algorithm can find what resonates
  • Keep all Advantage+ creative enhancement toggles OFF. Auto-enhancements convert worse
  • Let it run a minimum of 72 hours before making any changes
  • Once the platform identifies 1-3 winning combinations, survey buyers and talk to the sales team to extract real buying reasons
  • Transition to Strategy B once you have identified distinct audience pockets with proven hooks and conversion volume hits 10+ per week
CSM Watchout: At $30-$50/day, 30 days is $900-$1500/month. That is a tiny test budget. It takes weeks to accumulate enough data to make decisions. Do not let the client panic and start tweaking after 2 days. Set expectations on timeline before they start.

When to use: Clients spending $300-$1K/day with 10+ weekly conversions and proven hooks, or $1K-$3K/day in scaling mode. Also available at $100-$300/day if the client has crossed the borderline spend threshold and wants more control over budget distribution. This is the standard scaling structure. You have enough conversion volume to read data, you have proven winners, and you want control over budget distribution rather than letting the platform bias spend to the cheapest result.

When to use: Clients spending $1K+/day who need rapid creative testing, or any scaling account that needs to cycle through a high volume of creatives to find new winners fast. Thunderdome forces even spend distribution across every creative so you can read exactly how each one performs, then cut losers aggressively and turn winners into scaling assets.

Do not run Thunderdome below $1K/day.

Platform Trust Modifier: Override the Decision Tree When Trust Drops

Regardless of what the decision tree recommends, if platform trust is low, switch to maximum control settings. Reassess weekly.

High Trust Period

Stable platform, no major algorithm changes, no new auto-enabled toggles, no inflationary windows (holidays, Q4 surges).

Settings: Automated bidding permitted. CBO permitted. Dynamic Creative permitted. Automated placements permitted. Platform toggles can stay on.

Low Trust Period

Major algorithm rollout, new auto-enabled toggles that alter creative or destinations, inflationary windows like holidays, platform rep pushing settings that conflict with cost control.

Settings: Cost caps on every campaign. ABO only, no CBO. Individual ads only, no Dynamic Creative. Manual placement review. All enhancement and destination-optimization toggles OFF. Full sweep of every ad before publishing.

Core Decision Rules for CSMs

These rules override the flowchart when edge cases come up on calls.

Rule 1: Conversion Volume Beats Spend
If a client is spending $1K/day but only getting 5 conversions a week, they do not have enough signal for ABO or Thunderdome. Keep them on CBO until conversion volume hits 10+ per week. Spend alone does not unlock advanced structures. Data does.
Rule 2: Never Add to a Working Campaign
You can remove things from an existing campaign without issue. But adding creatives, targeting, or placements to a campaign that already has reach and performance will break it. If the client needs new creatives, relaunch the ad set or start a new campaign.
Rule 3: 72 Hours Minimum Before Judging
No creative decisions before 72 hours of runtime. Two hours is not signal. The only exception is if something is obviously broken: wrong link, rejected tracking, or CPMs completely out of range.
Rule 4: Relaunch Ad Sets, Not Campaigns
When ads fatigue, relaunch the ad set, not the campaign. Strip out the 1-3 fatigued winners so the remaining ads get distribution. Because you launched with 25-30 ads, there are always other ads ready to rip.
Rule 5: Testing Campaign Becomes Scale Campaign
You do not need a separate scale campaign. The creative testing campaign converts into the scaling campaign once winners are identified. The only time you run a separate structure is Thunderdome alongside ABO at $3K+/day.
Rule 6: Trust Level Overrides Everything
If the platform is in a low-trust period, force maximum control regardless of what the tree says: cost caps, ABO, individual ads, manual placements, all toggles off. Reassess weekly.
Rule 7: $100/day Is the Splitting Floor
Below $100/day, do not split between multiple ad sets. Above $100/day, splitting becomes viable and ABO becomes available. This is the borderline threshold for structural complexity.
Rule 8: Lookalikes Need Purchaser Data
Do not add a lookalike stack until you have enough purchaser data for the platform to model from. At $100-$300/day with under 10 conversions/week, stick to broad + interest. Lookalikes come after you have proven buyers.
Rule 9: Dynamic Creative Is Optional, Not Default
Dynamic Creative is an add-on to CBO, not a mandatory pairing. Use it when you want the platform to mix and match. Skip it when you have proven creatives or when platform trust is low. Never use it during low-trust periods.
Rule 10: CPA Benchmarks Are Guardrails, Not Targets
Use the cost-per-call benchmarks to know when something is off, not to hit a specific number. If CPA climbs but show rate holds, it is auction or fatigue. If CPA climbs and show rate drops, the creative is attracting the wrong people. Diagnose the sub-metric before changing anything.

Ad Testing to Combat Fatigue: By Spend Level

Every campaign will fatigue. The question is not if but when, and whether you have a system to replace winners before the account bleeds. Here is how to test new creatives at each spend level to keep feeding winners into the scaling structure.

How to Know It Is Time to Test New Creatives

Before you test, you need to know when to test. Ad fatigue is not just frequency getting high. The platform communicates through signals. Here is what to watch for across all spend levels:

Cost Per Result
Climbing over 2+ weeks with no campaign changes. The primary signal.
CTR (Link Click-Through)
Dropping. Your creative is losing its ability to stop the scroll.
Frequency
Climbing above 2.0 at low reach. The audience has seen the ads too many times.
Result Volume
Declining alongside rising cost. Both moving the wrong direction at once.
CPM
Inflating with no auction explanation. The platform is charging more for the same audience.
Call Quality
Sales team reports leads are less qualified. The creative is attracting the wrong segment.
The rule: Do not judge fatigue off frequency alone. Frequency climbing is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Judge fatigue off cost per result and result volume moving together. If cost is climbing but volume is holding, you may have audience fatigue, not ad fatigue. Run the ad fatigue protocol first: relaunch with fresh creatives. If cost does not recover after the relaunch, you have audience fatigue and need to expand your audience, not just swap creatives.

The Ad Fatigue Protocol (Applies at Every Spend Level)

Before diving into spend-specific testing approaches, here is the universal protocol. Every level uses a version of this. The only thing that changes by spend level is how many creatives you test and whether you run the test inside the existing campaign or in a separate one.

  • Duplicate the fatigued campaign or ad set. Do not edit the live one
  • Go to the ad level of the duplicated campaign
  • Pull out the fatigued videos or images
  • Put in fresh creatives from your surplus. New hooks, new angles, new testimonials
  • Press publish. Once approved, turn the old campaign off
  • Do not run both the old and new campaign at the same time. That splits your audience and your budget
  • After the fresh campaign launches, give it 72 hours before judging. Do not compare hour-one of the new campaign against the fatigued campaign's last week
Why duplicate, not edit: If you edit the live campaign, the platform resets the learning phase. You lose the accumulated audience data and the algorithm starts from scratch. Duplicating preserves the original as a backup and gives the new campaign a clean start without disrupting the old one's data.

The Creative Surplus Principle

You do not want to be one of those tweakers who immediately throws new creatives into the algorithm the second they are filmed. You want a surplus ready at all times. The bigger the budget, the more surplus you need. The lower the budget, the less you can test, but you still need a surplus for when fatigue hits.

$30-$50/day
Keep 5-10 untested creatives on the shelf at all times. You test 1-3 at a time. When they fatigue, you have replacements ready.
Under $100/day
Keep 10-15 untested creatives ready. You test 5-10 at a time.
$100-$300/day
Keep 15-20 untested creatives ready. You test 10-15 at a time across 2-3 ad sets.
$300-$1K/day
Keep 20-30 untested creatives ready. You test 15-20+ at a time.
$1K-$3K/day
Keep 30-50 untested creatives ready. Thunderdome tests 15-30+ at a time in parallel.
$3K+/day
Keep 50+ untested creatives in the pipeline at all times. Thunderdome cycles through them continuously. Film weekly to maintain the surplus.
The rule: The bigger the budget, the more I can test. The lower the budget, the less I can test. At $30/day you can test far fewer variables than at $3K/day. But the principle is the same at every level: always have a surplus ready before fatigue hits. If you wait until fatigue to film new creatives, you are already late. The creative surplus is your insurance policy against revenue dips.

The Test-to-Scale Cycle

Testing and scaling are not separate phases. They are a cycle. You test, you find winners, you scale, you hit a ceiling, you test again. The goal is to maintain account consolidation: spend more money, hit the ceiling, then go back and test again without breaking what is working.

  • Phase 1: Test. Load a batch of creatives into your testing structure (at low spend, this is the same campaign. At high spend, this is Thunderdome). Give it 72 hours minimum to read data. Let the platform find 1-3 winners
  • Phase 2: Identify. Once the platform has spent enough to show you which 1-3 ads are absorbing the spend and producing results, those are your winners. Do not tweak them. Do not touch them. Let them run
  • Phase 3: Scale. Increase budget on the winners. If on CBO, raise the campaign budget. If on ABO, raise the ad set budgets. Scale aggressively but watch for the ceiling
  • Phase 4: Hit the ceiling. Eventually the audience saturates. Cost per result climbs. Volume drops. This is the ceiling. Do not keep scaling past it. Pull back spend to the last profitable level
  • Phase 5: Test again. Go back to Phase 1 with a fresh batch of creatives. The testing campaign becomes the scaling campaign. You do not need a separate scale campaign unless you are running Thunderdome alongside ABO at $3K+/day
The key insight: Once you stop testing and the campaign becomes solely an accumulation of winning ad sets, your costs fall. The winners accumulate at a lower conversion cost than the losers. That is why your losers are losers. The more you test losers, the higher your costs seem. Once you stop spending on losers and let the winners run, costs drop. But then the winners fatigue, and you need to test again. That is the cycle.

How to Feed Winners from Test to Scale

Once your testing structure identifies winners, here is how to move them into the scaling structure without breaking what is already working. The approach differs by spend level:

$30-$300/day
The test campaign IS the scale campaign. Once the platform finds 1-3 winners, simply raise the budget on that campaign. Do not build a separate scale campaign. The winners are already running. Just feed them more budget.
$300-$1K/day
Same logic. The ABO testing campaign becomes the scaling campaign. Once winners are identified and you are past the testing phase, raise ad set budgets. Do not start a new campaign. The winners are already in the structure that found them.
$1K-$3K/day
Thunderdome finds the winners. Pull the winning creatives out of Thunderdome and load them as new ads in the ABO scaling campaign. Do not add them to existing ABO ad sets. Either relaunch the ABO ad set with the new winner included, or create a new ad set within the ABO campaign for the winner.
$3K+/day
Thunderdome runs permanently and feeds winners into ABO continuously. When an ABO ad set fatigues, relaunch it with Thunderdome winners already loaded. The system is self-sustaining: Thunderdome tests, ABO scales, Thunderdome replaces ABO's fatigued creatives.
Do not add to a working campaign. You can remove things from an existing campaign without issue. But adding creatives, targeting, or placements to a campaign that already has reach and performance will break it. If you need to add new winners to an ABO campaign, relaunch the ad set with the new winner included. Do not just drop new ads into the live ad set.

Common Testing Mistakes by Spend Level

$30-$50/day: Do not run a parallel test campaign. You do not have enough budget. Test within the one campaign you have.

Under $100/day: Do not split into multiple ad sets for testing. Stay consolidated in one ad set. The budget is too thin to read data across multiple ad sets.

$100-$300/day: Do not add creatives to a working ad set to test. Relaunch the ad set with new creatives. Adding breaks the performance.

$300-$1K/day: Do not run full Thunderdome below $1K/day. The budget per ad set is too thin. Each ad set at 15-30 would only get $10-30/day. Not enough signal to read.

$1K-$3K/day: Do not stop Thunderdome once it finds winners. Thunderdome runs continuously. It is not a one-time test. It is a permanent creative factory.

$3K+/day: Do not allocate more than 30% of total spend to Thunderdome. The majority goes to the stable ABO scaling campaign. Thunderdome is the R&D arm, not the revenue arm.
